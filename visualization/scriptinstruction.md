# Script Writing Instructions

## Audience
Architecture background — professors and students. They value process, embodiment, spatial thinking, and intellectual honesty. They are skeptical of tech hype.

## Tone
Conversational. Like a person talking, not a product launch. Light self-deprecation is fine. Admitting uncertainty is fine. Dramatic builds to nothing are not.

## What to keep
- **Rhythm phrases and breath-control sentences**: "First, I want to ask you a question." / "I mean, the actual texture of it." / "First, let me explain several concepts." These regulate pacing and are not waste.
- **The presenter's own phrasing**: Do not alter words or sentences the presenter wrote unless explicitly asked.
- **Sharp points stated plainly**: A strong observation does not need an example to explain it. Say it and move on.

## Do not stylize at the expense of naturalness
- Do not drop sentence starters like "This is" or "It is" to sound minimal. If the full sentence is the natural way to say it, write the full sentence.
- Do not fragment sentences into floating phrases for stylistic effect. A complete sentence is always preferred over an artificially truncated one.
- Do not use inversion or contrast structures to create a dramatic effect if the natural word order is clearer. e.g. don't put "nothing else" before "what I gave it" just to manufacture surprise.
- The standard is not "does this sentence read well" — it is "would this person actually say this out loud." Script is speech, not prose.
- Follow the natural order of events: what existed → what was done → what happened. Don't reorder for rhetorical effect.

## What to cut
- Hollow summary sentences that restate what was just shown: *"This is a fundamentally different kind of relationship."*
- Transition sentences that sound meaningful but carry no information: *"Designers in this room might recognize a version of this problem too."*
- Salesy or keynote-style drama: *"Whatever your benchmark for rapid growth, this exceeds it."* / *"It blanketed the planet."*
- The same point said twice in different words: *"Every conversation starts from scratch. / It has no memory of you."* — pick one, cut the other.

## What NOT to cut
- Content that appears for the first time, even if the sentence is long. Length is not the same as redundancy.
- Explanation that actually does work — listing labels or adjectives (e.g. "reactive, session-based") is not the same as explaining. If the script only labels without explaining, rewrite; do not cut.
- The distinction: brevity removes words. Efficiency removes redundancy. Only cut when information repeats, not when it fills space that needs filling.

## Content rules
- Data and statistics must be defensible. If uncertain, verify before using. If unverifiable, cut or qualify.
- General angle first, domain-specific angle second. Architecture is a bonus, not the premise.
- Do not over-explain. The audience is intelligent.

## Structure
- Each slide's script should do one thing and exit.
- If a slide is visual, the script can be short — let the image carry weight.
- Transitions between sections should feel like breathing, not like chapter headings being announced.
